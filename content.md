# SLIDE 2 — HOW DOES ATS WORK?

## HOW DOES YOUR RESUME GET SCREENED?

Before your resume reaches a recruiter, it may first pass through an **Applicant Tracking System (ATS).**

### 01 — READ

Extracts information from your resume

### 02 — PARSE

Identifies skills, experience, education and other sections

### 03 — MATCH

Compares your resume with the requirements of a job

### 04 — FILTER / RANK

Helps recruiters organize candidates based on configured criteria

### THE KEY IDEA

**Your first challenge isn't getting the interview.
It's making sure your resume clearly communicates your relevance.**

# SLIDE 4 — WHY RESUMES GET MISSED

## 4 COMMON RESUME PROBLEMS

### 01 — GENERIC LANGUAGE

**"Good at coding"**

Instead:

**Java · Spring Boot · REST APIs**

### 02 — MISSING JOB-SPECIFIC TERMS

The job description asks for **AWS + Docker + Git**, but your resume doesn't clearly mention them.

### 03 — HARD-TO-PARSE FORMATTING

Complex layouts, graphics, tables or unusual structures can make information harder to process.

### 04 — ONE RESUME FOR EVERY JOB

Different jobs emphasize different skills.

### THE GOAL

**Your resume should communicate the match — not make the recruiter search for it.**

# SLIDE 11 — YOUR 5 RESUME RULES

## YOUR 5 RESUME RULES

### 01

**TAILOR YOUR RESUME TO THE ROLE**

One job ≠ one generic resume.

### 02

**USE RELEVANT TERMS FROM THE JOB DESCRIPTION**

If you genuinely have the skill, make it visible.

### 03

**SHOW EVIDENCE, NOT EMPTY CLAIMS**

Instead of:

> "Good programmer"

> "Built X using Java and Spring Boot."

**KEEP THE STRUCTURE SIMPLE**

Make your resume easy for both software and humans to process.

### 05

**CHECK BEFORE YOU APPLY**

Ask:

> **"Does this resume clearly demonstrate why I'm relevant to this job?"**
